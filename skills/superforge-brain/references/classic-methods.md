# Classic Methods — the Lighter Alternative to a Full Sweep

BreakBias is not the only way to generate ideas, and it is not always the
right one. It exists for the moment novelty and defensibility matter enough
to justify a tracked, exhaustive sweep. Most brainstorming is not that moment
— a first pass, a low-stakes feature list, or a workshop where the point is
participation rather than proof, is usually better served by a method that
takes minutes, not a cell ledger.

**Offer the choice explicitly, once, before generating anything** — see
`SKILL.md` §1. Do not default silently to the heavier engine just because it
is this skill's namesake, and do not talk the user out of the lighter option
if that is what the situation calls for.

---

## The menu

| Method | What it does | Best for | Weak at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Classic brainstorming** (Osborn's rules) | Free generation under four rules: no criticism during generation, quantity over quality, wild ideas welcome, combine and build on others' | fast divergence, a group that already trusts each other | premature convergence — the first plausible idea usually ends the session |
| **SCAMPER** | Run the subject through 7 lenses: **S**ubstitute, **C**ombine, **A**dapt, **M**odify, **P**ut to another use, **E**liminate, **R**everse | a fast structured pass on an existing product, one person or a small group | shallower than BreakBias's sub-methods — one idea per lens, not a sweep |
| **Mind Mapping** | Radial diagram from a central concept, branches expand associatively, sub-branches go as deep as useful | exploring a space before the shape of the problem is clear | not a generation method by itself — good for scoping, weak for forcing novelty |
| **Six Thinking Hats** (de Bono) | The same idea examined from six parallel perspectives: facts, gut feeling, caution, optimism, creative alternatives, process | surfacing objections and blind spots on one idea already on the table | not a divergence method — use it to stress-test, not to generate |
| **Crazy 8s** | 8 ideas in 8 minutes, one per fold of a sheet, forced sketch-speed | breaking fixation fast, individual or small group, visual/UI ideas | quantity over depth — treat outputs as seeds, not finished concepts |
| **How Might We (HMW)** | Reframe a problem statement as a generative question — "How might we make the wait feel shorter?" — then brainstorm answers to the question | when the problem itself is still vague and needs reframing before generating solutions | only as good as the reframe; a bad HMW question produces a confidently wrong sweep |
| **Brainwriting (6-3-5)** | Silent written generation: each person writes 3 ideas in 5 minutes, passes the sheet, the next person builds on what is there. No talking during the round | groups where the loudest voice usually wins the room | needs multiple participants to work as designed; degrades to solo writing with one person |
| **Reverse brainstorming** | Ask "how would we guarantee this fails / makes the problem worse", generate freely, then invert each answer into a solution | when direct brainstorming keeps producing the same three obvious answers | inversion sometimes produces nonsense — expect a lower hit rate, generate more raw material than usual |
| **Random input** | Introduce an unrelated word or object, force an associative link back to the subject, and see what the forced connection surfaces | breaking a specific fixation once other methods keep circling the same idea | can feel arbitrary if the group has not bought into the method first |

## Running a classic-method session

1. **Pick one method**, or offer the menu and let the user pick. Do not blend
   more than two in a single sitting — the point of each is a specific kind of
   pressure on the thinking, and stacking them dilutes that.
2. **State the rules of the chosen method before generating**, in one line —
   "no criticism until we stop" for classic brainstorming, the 7 letters for
   SCAMPER, the reframed question for HMW.
3. **Generate without pruning**, same as BreakBias — kill and rank after the
   list exists, never during.
4. **No ledger, no kill codes, no blind judging.** These are what make
   BreakBias exhaustive and defensible; a classic-method session trades that
   away on purpose for speed. Say so if the user later wants a "which one is
   really the best" answer — that question needs the fuller protocol, not a
   quick pass re-read for scores it was never built to support.
5. **A short synthesis, not a full artifact.** Cluster what came out, name the
   two or three worth carrying forward, and note whether the result feels
   thin enough to warrant re-running as a full BreakBias sweep.

## When to upgrade to BreakBias mid-session

If a classic-method pass keeps producing the same three shapes, or the
decision behind it turns out to matter more than it looked like at the start,
say so and offer the upgrade rather than continuing to force a fast method
past its useful range. The ideas already generated are not wasted — they
become the "obvious three" (or more) that BreakBias bans before its own sweep
starts.
